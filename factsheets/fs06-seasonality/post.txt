Do stocks repeat their best calendar month? Heston & Sadka (2008) found that a stock that did well in March tends to do well in March again, for up to 20 years. It became one of the better-documented anomalies.

I tested it on blue-chip stocks, 2013–2026, in the US, the UK and continental Europe (plus Denmark, Scandinavia and a combined world book), using the index members of the time and after costs.

The result: it lost money in 5 of 6 markets after costs. Denmark is clearly negative; the US and Europe sit right at the edge of significance. Before costs there was no reliable edge: -0.7% a year across the US, UK and Europe (t -0.29). Most of the loss is the cost of trading the signal at about 80% turnover a month.

I checked the signal itself: requiring five years of history or using only the 12-month lag gives no reliable premium in any market, and shifting it by a month gives no reliable premium either.

Earlier data are mixed. In 1998–2013 the portfolio spread was also small (t +0.4; US 1999–2012 t +1.16), even though the cross-sectional slope was strongly positive (t +4.33). None of the pre-declared fixes rescued it, and none of its 12 cells passes a gate corrected for the 825 trials in this series.

Has anyone seen calendar-month seasonality survive costs in large caps recently? I would like to compare notes.
