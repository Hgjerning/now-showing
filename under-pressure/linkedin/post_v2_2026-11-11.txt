A 50-year-old balance-sheet score versus one number from the price chart. The price chart won, by a mile.

I tested which number best spots next year's crashes (a fall of 40% or more) in the S&P 500, 2012–2025, with the test written down before I ran it:

• Last year's volatility: 0.79
• Piotroski F: 0.61
• Altman Z: 0.60
• Ohlson O: 0.53 (0.50 is a coin flip)

Sort the index on volatility and the crash rate climbs like a staircase: about 1% in the calmest tenth, nearly 1 in 4 in the wildest. Sort on Ohlson's O and the line is flat.

And once you know the volatility, adding all three accounting scores did not improve the ranking out of sample.

The takeaway: if you want to know which stocks can fall hard, start with the price chart. It is free, it updates daily, and it beat each balance-sheet score in at least 48 of the 50 quarters with a crash.

Fair caveat: Altman and Ohlson built their scores to predict bankruptcy, not price crashes. Altman's Z still beat chance, just not volatility.

Do you use a distress score in your process, and what does it add that the price chart doesn't?
