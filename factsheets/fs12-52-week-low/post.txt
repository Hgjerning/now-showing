Folklore says stocks at their yearly low are bargains: 'buy low'. The academic evidence points the other way: stocks near their lows tend to keep underperforming (George & Hwang 2004), and in the series A10 Free Fallin' found knives rebound only in market-wide crashes.

I tested it on six markets, 2013–2026: US, Europe, UK, Denmark, Scandinavia and the world. Point-in-time index members, after costs.

Long/short (stocks closest to their 52-week low minus stocks furthest above it): -14.7% to -5.8% a year, positive in none of the six markets. Adjusted for market beta: -15.8% to -7.3%.
Long-only (stocks closest to their 52-week low): a better Sharpe than owning all the stocks equally in none of the six markets.

The pre-declared fixes (beta-neutral legs, a turnover buffer, volatility targeting) took the 2020–26 holdout Sharpe from -0.78 to -0.57.

360° view: once its nearest neighbours are in the model, nothing is left: the signal is redundant.
